## Assigned ground: the decision as it stood at the time

Argue from what the actor could know and do at the moment of the act: the information
available then, the constraints in force, the alternatives genuinely open, and the urgency
involved. Assess the choice by the situation facing the actor, not by how it turned out.

Do not argue from consequences. Another advocate on your side covers that ground, and
duplicating it wastes the panel's attention. If the charge sheet says nothing about what
was known at the time, say so plainly and argue from what it does establish.
